# Summa v0.1 Requirements

## Purpose

Summa v0.1 is intended to provide a narrow, usable financial operating core for Echelon Foundry without attempting to replicate QuickBooks.

The initial goal is:

> Allow Echelon Foundry to maintain a correct double-entry ledger, create and issue invoices, track what clients owe, record payments, and produce the core reports needed to understand the business.

The first version should remain deliberately constrained. The objective is to prove that a tightly integrated ledger, invoicing, receivables, and payment-recording system can replace the portion of QuickBooks that Echelon actually needs right now.

---

## 1. General Ledger

The ledger is the accounting source of truth.

### 1.1 Accounts

The system shall maintain a chart of accounts.

Each account shall have:

- Unique ID
- Account number or code
- Name
- Account type
- Active/inactive status

Supported account types shall initially be:

- Asset
- Liability
- Equity
- Revenue
- Expense

The system shall understand each account type's normal debit/credit balance.

### 1.2 Journal Entries

The system shall support journal entries containing two or more journal lines.

Each journal line shall contain:

- Account
- Debit or credit amount
- Optional memo
- Optional dimensions/reference information

Rules:

- Total debits must equal total credits before an entry may be posted.
- Debit and credit values must use fixed decimal currency representation, never floating-point numbers.
- A journal line may not simultaneously contain both a debit and a credit.
- Negative debit/credit values shall not be permitted.

### 1.3 Journal Entry States

```text
Draft
  ↓
Balanced
  ↓
Posted
  ↓
Reversed
```

Requirements:

- Draft entries may be edited.
- Posted entries shall be immutable.
- Posted entries shall not be deleted.
- Corrections to posted entries shall be made through reversal or correcting entries.
- Every posted entry shall have an immutable unique identifier.

---

## 2. Accounting Periods

Requirements:

- Transactions shall have an effective accounting date.
- The system shall support accounting periods.
- Periods may be:
  - Open
  - Closed
  - Locked
- New entries may only be posted into an open period.
- Closing or locking a period shall not modify existing entries.
- Reopening a period should require an explicit privileged operation.

For v0.1, monthly periods are sufficient.

---

## 3. Customers

The system shall maintain customer/client records.

Minimum data:

```text
Customer
  Id
  Name
  BillingName
  BillingAddress
  Email
  DefaultPaymentTerms
  Active
```

Customer management should remain narrowly focused on billing and financial operations.

CRM functionality is out of scope.

---

## 4. Invoices

The system shall support creation of customer invoices.

An invoice shall contain:

```text
Invoice
  Id
  InvoiceNumber
  Customer
  IssueDate
  DueDate
  Status
  Currency
  LineItems
  Subtotal
  Adjustments
  Total
  AmountPaid
  AmountOutstanding
```

An invoice line shall contain:

```text
InvoiceLine
  Description
  Quantity
  UnitPrice
  Amount
  RevenueAccount
  OptionalProject
  OptionalWorkItem
```

### 4.1 Invoice States

Primary lifecycle:

```text
Draft
  ↓
Issued
  ↓
PartiallyPaid
  ↓
Paid
```

Payment timing classification should remain orthogonal:

```text
Current
Overdue
```

`Overdue` should not replace `Issued`.

An invoice may conceptually be both issued and overdue.

---

## 5. Invoice Numbering

Requirements:

- Invoice numbers shall be unique.
- Invoice numbering shall be deterministic and sequential unless manually overridden.
- Once an invoice is issued, its invoice number shall not change.
- Draft invoices may receive a temporary internal ID before being issued.

Example:

```text
EF-2026-0001
```

---

## 6. Issuing an Invoice

Issuing an invoice shall be an explicit business transition.

Requirements:

- A draft invoice must contain at least one line.
- The customer must exist and be active.
- The invoice total must be greater than zero.
- A valid revenue account must exist for each invoice line.
- The due date shall be calculated from payment terms unless explicitly provided.
- Issuing an invoice shall create and post the corresponding accounting entry.

Example:

```text
Invoice: $6,050

Debit   Accounts Receivable   $6,050
Credit  Consulting Revenue    $6,050
```

This should occur as one logical operation.

### 6.1 Invariant

> An issued invoice cannot exist without its corresponding posted journal entry.

Retrying the operation must not accidentally create duplicate invoices, obligations, or journal entries.

Idempotency is required from the beginning.

---

## 7. Invoice Output

Summa shall produce a presentable customer invoice.

Initial output requirements:

- HTML output
- Printable to PDF
- Company name and contact information
- Customer billing information
- Invoice number
- Issue date
- Due date
- Payment terms
- Line-item descriptions
- Quantity/rate where applicable
- Total due
- Payment instructions

A custom PDF rendering engine is out of scope for v0.1.

The initial approach should use clean HTML and CSS that prints well to PDF.

---

## 8. Sending Invoices

For the initial version:

- The system shall track whether an invoice has been issued.
- The system shall record when it was sent.
- The system shall record the recipient email address.
- The system may initially allow manual sending outside Summa.

Suggested metadata:

```text
IssuedAt
SentAt
SentTo
```

Direct Gmail or email integration should be deferred until the core billing flow is stable.

---

## 9. Payment Terms and Due Dates

Supported initial terms:

```text
Due on receipt
Net 7
Net 15
Net 30
Net 45
Net 60
Custom date
```

Requirements:

- The system shall automatically derive a due date based on the selected terms.
- The system shall calculate, rather than store:
  - DaysUntilDue
  - DaysPastDue

---

## 10. Receivables / Obligations

Receivables should be a first-class concept rather than merely a presentation of invoices.

When an invoice is issued:

```text
Invoice
   ↓
Receivable Obligation
```

Minimum model:

```text
Obligation
  Id
  Source
  Party
  OriginalAmount
  OutstandingAmount
  DueDate
  Status
```

States:

```text
Open
PartiallySettled
Settled
Cancelled
```

Derived classifications:

```text
Current
DueSoon
Overdue
```

The obligation model should be designed so it can later support payables without requiring a redesign.

---

## 11. Payments

Summa shall record customer payments.

Minimum data:

```text
Payment
  Id
  Customer
  DateReceived
  Amount
  Method
  Reference
  Memo
```

Initial payment methods:

```text
ACH
Check
Wire
CreditCard
Cash
Other
```

Payment processing itself is not required for v0.1.

The system only needs to record that a payment occurred.

---

## 12. Payment Allocation

Payments and invoices shall not have a simple one-to-one relationship.

A payment may cover:

```text
One invoice
Multiple invoices
Part of an invoice
```

A single invoice may also receive multiple payments.

Model:

```text
PaymentAllocation
  PaymentId
  InvoiceId
  Amount
```

Requirements:

- Allocations may not exceed the unallocated payment balance.
- Allocations may not exceed the invoice outstanding balance.
- Multiple allocations may exist for one payment.
- Multiple payments may apply to one invoice.

---

## 13. Accounting for Payments

When a payment is recorded and allocated, the system shall create the appropriate journal entry.

Example:

```text
Debit   Cash / Bank Account
Credit  Accounts Receivable
```

The posting amount should correspond to the payment allocation.

An invoice becomes `Paid` only when:

```text
OutstandingAmount = 0
```

The invoice status should be a consequence of financial facts, not a manually editable value.

### 13.1 General Principle

> Important financial states should be consequences of facts, not editable fields.

---

## 14. Accounts Receivable Aging

Summa shall provide an AR aging view.

Initial aging buckets:

```text
Current
1-30 days
31-60 days
61-90 days
90+ days
```

The report should show at least:

| Customer | Current | 1-30 | 31-60 | 61-90 | 90+ | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

The report should allow drill-down to the underlying invoices.

---

## 15. Core Financial Reports

For v0.1, Summa shall support the following reports.

### 15.1 Trial Balance

```text
Account
Debit Balance
Credit Balance
```

Required invariant:

```text
Total Debits = Total Credits
```

### 15.2 General Ledger

For each account:

```text
Date
Entry
Description
Debit
Credit
Running Balance
```

### 15.3 Income Statement

For a selected date range:

```text
Revenue
- Expenses
= Net Income
```

### 15.4 Balance Sheet

As of a selected date:

```text
Assets
= Liabilities + Equity
```

Cash flow reporting may be deferred until the accounting model is more mature.

---

## 16. Dimensions

Dimensions should be supported from the beginning, even if the initial UI exposes them minimally.

A journal line may optionally reference:

```text
Client
Project
Engagement
WorkItem
Product
```

These dimensions should not be modeled as separate ledger accounts.

Example:

```text
Consulting Revenue
  Client = ABC
  Engagement = Architecture Assessment
```

Avoid structures such as:

```text
Consulting Revenue - ABC - Architecture Assessment
```

Dimensions will become valuable when Summa integrates with ROS and Time Entry.

---

## 17. Time Entry Integration Boundary

The integration boundary with Time Entry should be defined now, even if not implemented in v0.1.

Proposed future flow:

```text
Approved Billable Time
        ↓
Invoice Proposal
        ↓
Human Review
        ↓
Invoice
```

A time entry should carry enough context that an invoice line can trace back to the work that generated it.

Approved time should not automatically produce a customer invoice.

The system should generate an invoice proposal for human review.

---

## 18. Audit Trail

Every material operation shall record:

```text
Who
What
When
Source
CorrelationId
```

At minimum, audit the following actions:

- Journal entry created
- Journal entry posted
- Journal entry reversed
- Invoice created
- Invoice changed
- Invoice issued
- Invoice sent
- Payment recorded
- Payment allocated
- Accounting period closed
- Accounting period reopened

Audit records shall not be editable.

---

## 19. Idempotency

Idempotency is a core technical requirement.

Operations such as:

```text
IssueInvoice
RecordPayment
PostJournalEntry
ImportTime
```

must tolerate retries safely.

For example, if an agent or API retries:

```text
IssueInvoice(INV-001)
```

five times, there should still be:

```text
1 invoice
1 AR obligation
1 journal entry
```

and not five of each.

This is especially important for future agent, workflow, and GitHub integrations.

---

## 20. Data Export and Escape Hatch

Summa shall always support exporting core business and accounting data.

At minimum:

```text
Chart of Accounts
Journal Entries
General Ledger
Trial Balance
Invoices
Customers
Payments
AR Aging
```

Initial export format:

- CSV

Future format:

- JSON

The purpose of this requirement is to avoid lock-in.

If Summa is abandoned or replaced, the business must be able to migrate its records into another accounting system without reconstructing its financial history manually.

---

# Explicitly Out of Scope for v0.1

The following capabilities should not be built in the first version:

- Payroll
- Employee expense reimbursement
- Inventory
- Purchase orders
- Full accounts payable
- Credit-card processing
- ACH processing
- Bank feeds
- Automatic bank reconciliation
- Sales tax calculation
- Tax preparation
- 1099 preparation
- W-2 preparation
- Multi-company consolidation
- Multi-currency accounting
- Budgeting
- Forecasting
- Depreciation automation
- Fixed-asset management
- Recurring invoices
- Automatic collection emails
- Mobile application
- Complex role-based permissions

These capabilities may be considered later if actual business needs justify them.

# First Vertical Slice

Before implementing all of v0.1, the first SDE vertical slice should prove the following end-to-end workflow:

```text
Create client
    ↓
Enter one consulting invoice
    ↓
Issue invoice
    ↓
Generate printable invoice
    ↓
Automatically post AR / Revenue
    ↓
See invoice as outstanding
    ↓
Record payment
    ↓
Automatically post Cash / AR
    ↓
Invoice shows Paid
    ↓
Trial balance balances
    ↓
P&L and balance sheet reflect it
```

If this workflow works correctly, Summa is already capable of supporting a real consulting billing cycle.

# Design Principles

## Ledger Is the Financial Source of Truth

The ledger records completed financial events.

Balances should be derived from posted journal entries rather than treated as independently editable values.

## Obligations Represent Unfinished Business

The obligation system tracks what still needs to happen financially.

Examples include:

- Customer payments that are due
- Future payables
- Other financial deadlines

## Posted Records Are Immutable

Posted journal entries and issued financial facts should not be silently altered.

Corrections should be explicit and auditable.

## Financial States Are Derived

Whenever possible, important states such as `Paid`, `Overdue`, and balances should be derived from underlying financial facts rather than manually entered.

## Integration Through Business Events

Other systems should integrate with Summa through explicit business operations and events.

Examples:

```text
Approved Billable Time
Invoice Issued
Payment Received
Journal Entry Posted
```

## Scope Discipline

Summa should not attempt to recreate QuickBooks.

The target is a narrowly integrated financial operating system that supports Echelon Foundry's actual needs while remaining simple enough to understand, verify, and maintain.

# Open Questions for the Next Requirements Pass

The next requirements round should challenge the current model using real-world scenarios, including:

- Partial payments
- One payment covering multiple invoices
- Retainers
- Customer deposits
- Credit memos
- Refunds
- Voided invoices
- Invoice corrections
- Write-offs
- Bad debt
- Reimbursable client expenses
- Hourly billing
- Fixed-price billing
- Milestone billing
- Discounts
- Overpayments
- Customer credits
- Year-end CPA adjustments
- Opening balances
- Closing entries
- Customer disputes
- Bounced or reversed payments

The purpose of the next round is not to expand scope indiscriminately, but to determine whether the core domain model remains sound when exposed to normal accounting edge cases.
