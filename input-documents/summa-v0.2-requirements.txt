# Summa v0.2 Requirements

## Billing and Accounting Edge Cases

The goal of this pass is:

> Prove that the core model survives normal business exceptions without corrupting the ledger, receivables, invoice state, or audit trail.

---

## 1. Partial Payments

The system shall support payments that are less than the outstanding invoice balance.

Example:

Invoice total:       $10,000
Payment received:     $4,000
Outstanding:          $6,000

Requirements:

- Invoice remains open.
- Invoice becomes PartiallyPaid.
- Receivable obligation remains open.
- Outstanding amount becomes $6,000.
- Ledger posts only $4,000 against AR.
- Subsequent payments may settle the remaining balance.

Invariant:

AmountPaid + AmountOutstanding = InvoiceTotal

---

## 2. Multiple Payments Against One Invoice

The system shall support multiple payments against the same invoice.

Example:

Invoice             $10,000

Payment 1            $2,000
Payment 2            $3,000
Payment 3            $5,000

Requirements:

- All allocations must remain traceable.
- The invoice becomes paid only when outstanding balance reaches zero.
- Payment history must remain visible after settlement.

---

## 3. One Payment Applied to Multiple Invoices

A single payment may be allocated across multiple invoices.

Example:

Payment: $12,000

INV-001   $5,000
INV-002   $4,000
INV-003   $3,000

Requirements:

- Allocation sum may not exceed payment amount.
- Allocation sum may be less than payment amount.
- Unallocated payment balances must remain visible.
- Each invoice updates independently.

---

## 4. Overpayments

The system shall support customer payments exceeding the amount allocated to invoices.

Example:

Invoice:     $5,000
Payment:     $6,000
Allocated:   $5,000
Unallocated: $1,000

The remaining $1,000 shall not become revenue automatically.

It should remain a customer credit or unapplied payment.

Proposed model:

CustomerCredit
  Customer
  Amount
  SourcePayment
  RemainingAmount

Accounting treatment initially:

Debit  Cash
Credit Accounts Receivable / Customer Credit Liability

Exact treatment should be configurable or reviewed before final implementation.

---

## 5. Customer Deposits and Retainers

The system shall support money received before revenue is earned.

Example:

Customer pays $10,000 deposit.
Work has not yet been performed.

This must not become revenue immediately.

Accounting treatment:

Debit  Cash
Credit Customer Deposits / Deferred Revenue

Later, when earned:

Debit  Deferred Revenue
Credit Consulting Revenue

Requirements:

- Deposits must remain distinguishable from invoice payments.
- Deposit balances must be traceable by customer.
- A deposit may later be applied against an invoice.

---

## 6. Invoice Corrections Before Issue

Draft invoices may be freely edited.

Changes may include:

- Customer
- Descriptions
- Quantities
- Rates
- Dates
- Terms
- Dimensions

No accounting entry should exist yet.

---

## 7. Invoice Corrections After Issue

Issued invoices shall not simply be edited in ways that rewrite financial history.

For material changes, Summa should require one of:

- Void and reissue
- Credit adjustment
- Correcting invoice

The original issued invoice shall remain visible.

Important rule:

> Issued financial records must preserve their historical state.

---

## 8. Voiding Invoices

An issued invoice may need to be voided.

Requirements:

- Invoice cannot simply be deleted.
- Original invoice remains in audit history.
- Related receivable must be cancelled or reversed.
- Related ledger entry must be reversed.
- Invoice state becomes Voided.

Example accounting:

Original:

Debit AR       5,000
Credit Revenue 5,000

Void:

Debit Revenue  5,000
Credit AR      5,000

---

## 9. Credit Memos

Summa shall support customer credits.

Example:

Original invoice      $10,000
Approved credit        $2,000
Remaining amount       $8,000

A credit memo shall:

- Have its own identifier
- Reference the originating customer
- Optionally reference the invoice
- Create appropriate accounting entries
- Reduce the outstanding customer balance

Suggested state:

Draft
Issued
Applied
FullyApplied
Voided

---

## 10. Refunds

The system shall distinguish:

Customer credit

from:

Cash refund

A refund should represent money actually leaving the business.

Example:

Debit  Customer Credit / AR
Credit Cash

Requirements:

- Refund shall reference the customer.
- Refund should optionally reference original payment/invoice.
- Refund shall be auditable.
- Refund shall never silently delete the original payment.

---

## 11. Bounced or Reversed Payments

Payments can fail after initially being recorded.

Examples:

- Bounced check
- ACH reversal
- Credit-card chargeback

Summa must support:

PaymentRecorded
    ↓
PaymentReversed

Requirements:

- Original payment remains in history.
- Reversal creates compensating journal entries.
- Invoice outstanding balance reopens appropriately.
- Receivable obligation returns to open status where necessary.

---

## 12. Write-Offs

The system shall support intentional write-off of uncollectible balances.

Example:

Invoice outstanding: $500
Decision: write off

Accounting:

Debit  Bad Debt Expense
Credit Accounts Receivable

Requirements:

- Write-off must be explicit.
- Reason should be recorded.
- Original invoice remains visible.
- Written-off amount remains reportable separately from collected revenue.

---

## 13. Discounts

Support at least:

- Line-item discount
- Invoice-level discount

Requirements:

- Discounts must be represented explicitly.
- The system must not silently modify unit prices if a discount was intended.
- Accounting treatment must remain deterministic.

For v0.2, percentage and fixed-amount discounts are sufficient.

---

## 14. Reimbursable Client Expenses

The system shall support expenses incurred on behalf of a client.

Examples:

- Travel
- Hosting
- Materials
- External services

Requirements:

- Expense may be associated with customer/project/engagement.
- Expense may be marked billable or non-billable.
- Billable expenses may be proposed for invoice inclusion.
- Original business expense and later customer reimbursement must remain separate accounting events.

Do not simply net them together.

---

## 15. Hourly Billing

Support invoice generation from billable time.

Time entry minimum data:

Person
Client
Project
Date
Hours
Rate
Description
Billable
Approved

Flow:

Approved Time
     ↓
Invoice Proposal
     ↓
Human Review
     ↓
Invoice

Requirements:

- Time should not disappear after invoicing.
- Invoice line should retain traceability to originating time entries.
- Time entries may not be invoiced twice.

---

## 16. Fixed-Price Billing

The system shall support invoices not tied to hours.

Example:

Architecture Assessment
Fixed fee: $15,000

Requirements:

- Fixed-price invoices may exist without time-entry linkage.
- Internal time tracking may still associate costs with the engagement.
- Revenue amount shall be based on the billing agreement, not hours worked.

This distinction matters because later profitability analysis may compare internal effort to fixed-fee revenue.

---

## 17. Milestone Billing

Support engagements such as:

30% kickoff
40% midpoint
30% completion

Requirements:

- Engagement may define billing milestones.
- Milestone invoice proposal may reference the engagement.
- Milestone completion and invoice issuance must remain separate actions.

---

## 18. Payment Terms per Customer and Invoice

Customers may have default payment terms.

Invoices may override them.

Priority:

Invoice-specific terms
    ↓
Customer default terms
    ↓
System default terms

All derived due dates must retain the original terms used.

---

## 19. Late Status

Overdue status shall be derived.

Example:

OutstandingAmount > 0
AND
CurrentDate > DueDate

Then:

Overdue = true

No user should manually mark an invoice overdue.

---

## 20. Receivable Aging Must Use Outstanding Amount

AR aging shall not use original invoice total.

Example:

Invoice total:       $10,000
Paid:                 $8,000
Outstanding:          $2,000

Only $2,000 belongs in aging.

---

## 21. Opening Balances

If Summa replaces or supplements an existing accounting system, it must support controlled opening balances.

Requirements:

- Opening balances shall be identifiable as such.
- Migration date shall be explicit.
- Summa shall not require recreation of every historical transaction.
- Opening trial balance must balance before posting.

Suggested source:

Source = OpeningBalanceMigration

---

## 22. CPA Adjustments

The system shall allow manual adjusting journal entries.

Typical examples:

- Depreciation
- Accruals
- Prepaid expense adjustments
- Tax adjustments
- Reclassifications

Requirements:

- Adjusting entries must be distinguishable from operational entries.
- Entry must identify who created it.
- Optional external reference should support CPA workpaper references.

---

## 23. Year-End Closing

The model shall support closing temporary accounts.

Eventually:

Revenue
Expenses
   ↓
Retained Earnings

For v0.2, do not automate this yet.

Requirement:

> The ledger design must not prevent formal year-end closing entries.

---

## 24. Cash vs Accrual Reporting

The authoritative ledger should remain accrual-based.

Example:

Invoice issued
    ↓
Revenue recognized

even before payment arrives.

Later Summa may provide a cash-basis reporting projection.

Do not maintain two competing ledgers.

---

## 25. Duplicate Detection

The system should protect against accidental duplication.

Potential duplicates:

- Same invoice
- Same payment
- Same imported time entry
- Same journal entry request

At minimum use:

- Idempotency keys
- External references
- Source identifiers
- Correlation IDs

A duplicate request should return the existing result rather than create another financial record.

---

## 26. Currency Precision

Use a decimal or fixed-precision type everywhere money is represented.

Never use:

- float
- double

for monetary amounts.

Requirements:

- Explicit currency
- Deterministic rounding
- Consistent rounding policy

For v0.2, USD-only is acceptable.

---

## 27. Date Semantics

Distinguish:

- CreatedAt
- OccurredAt
- AccountingDate
- IssuedAt
- DueDate
- PaidAt
- PostedAt

These are not interchangeable.

An agent should not collapse them into one generic Date field.

---

## 28. Audit Provenance

Every externally triggered financial operation should preserve origin.

Example:

SourceSystem = TimeEntry
SourceId = TE-9821
CorrelationId = ...
Actor = Kevin
Agent = ...

If an AI agent initiates an action, that fact should be preserved.

---

## 29. Permissions Boundary

Full RBAC is still out of scope, but Summa should distinguish capabilities such as:

- CreateDraftInvoice
- IssueInvoice
- RecordPayment
- PostJournalEntry
- ReverseJournalEntry
- ClosePeriod
- ReopenPeriod

This is preferable to generic CRUD permissions.

---

## 30. Agent Execution Safety

Since an agent will be building and eventually may operate against Summa, automated agents shall not be able to perform irreversible financial operations without satisfying domain rules.

Examples:

- IssueInvoice
- PostJournalEntry
- ReversePayment
- ClosePeriod
- WriteOffReceivable

These operations must use explicit commands with validation.

No direct database mutation should bypass domain transitions.

---

## 31. Integration Boundaries

Summa should expose domain-level operations rather than generic CRUD wherever possible.

Prefer:

- IssueInvoice
- RecordPayment
- AllocatePayment
- VoidInvoice
- ApplyCreditMemo
- ReversePayment
- CloseAccountingPeriod

over:

- PUT /invoice
- PATCH /payment
- DELETE /journal-entry

This keeps agents and future integrations inside legal business transitions.

---

Before v0.2 is considered complete, the implementation should prove at least these scenarios:

1. Full invoice payment
2. Partial payment
3. Multiple payments
4. One payment across multiple invoices
5. Customer overpayment
6. Deposit before invoicing
7. Invoice void
8. Credit memo
9. Refund
10. Payment reversal
11. Bad debt write-off
12. Hourly invoice
13. Fixed-fee invoice
14. Milestone invoice
15. Reimbursable expense
16. Overdue invoice aging
17. Opening balance migration
18. Manual CPA adjustment
19. Duplicate command retry

Each scenario should verify:

- Domain state
- Ledger entries
- Balances

- Idempotency

---

## Implementation Guidance

The agent should not interpret every scenario in this document as a requirement to implement every possible feature immediately.

This pass serves two purposes:

1. Some items become actual v0.2 capabilities.
2. Others prove that the domain model can support them later without redesign.

Higher-priority implementation candidates include:

- Partial payments
- Multiple payment allocations
- Invoice voids
- Credits
- Payment reversals
- Opening balances
- CPA adjustments

Capabilities that may be modeled now but implemented later include:

- Milestone billing
- Reimbursable expenses
- Retainers

The objective is to prevent domain-model mistakes without allowing Summa to expand into a full QuickBooks replacement prematurely.

---

## Architectural Principle

The system should preserve a strict separation between:

- Business facts
- Accounting consequences
- Outstanding obligations
- Derived status
- Audit provenance

Financial history should be append-oriented and explicit.

Important financial state transitions should occur through domain commands and validated state transitions rather than direct record editing.
